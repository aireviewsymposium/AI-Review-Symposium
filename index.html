The 1st Seminar on AI for Peer Review

at Oxford 2026

Date, 1 Aug 2026; Room, TBC


Introduction

This platform supports a research study investigating how artificial intelligence (AI) systems compare to human experts in academic peer review and editorial decision-making.

The study is led by Dr Lin Li (University of Oxford), under the supervision of Prof. Yarin Gal, and conducted in collaboration with Dr Jianing Qiu (MBZUAI). The goal is to better understand similarities and differences between human and AI evaluations of academic work, including review quality, scoring behaviour, and acceptance decisions.

As part of this study, invited participants will take part in a simulated peer review process modelled on standard practices at major AI conferences. Participants will act as either reviewers or Area Chairs (ACs), evaluating a small number of submitted papers via the OpenReview platform.

The results of this study will contribute to research on reliable and responsible AI systems, particularly in the context of AI-assisted scientific evaluation.


Call for Papers

We invite submissions of research papers to be evaluated as part of a controlled study on academic peer review. Submissions may cover topics broadly related to artificial intelligence and machine learning, including but not limited to:

1. Machine learning theory and methods

2. Deep learning and representation learning

3. Computer vision and natural language processing

4. AI safety, robustness, and reliability

5. Uncertainty quantification and trustworthy AI

6. Applications of AI in real-world domains

Submitted papers will be used as evaluation material for both human participants (reviewers and Area Chairs) and AI systems. The aim is to enable a structured comparison of reviewing and decision-making processes.
